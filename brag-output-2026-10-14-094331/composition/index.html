Question 1 · Inference

A university introduced an optional seminar for students who had already completed an introductory course. Although attendance was low, students who attended regularly performed better on the final assessment than those who did not. However, the seminar attracted a disproportionate number of students who had scored highly in the introductory course. The university is considering making the seminar compulsory, arguing that its apparent benefits justify wider participation.

Which of the following can be most reasonably inferred from the information above?

AThe seminar’s benefits were concentrated among students who had scored highly in the introductory course.●
BMaking the seminar compulsory would raise the final-assessment performance of students who previously chose not to attend.●
CThe better results of regular attenders may partly reflect the stronger preparation of those who chose to attend.●
DRegular attenders scored higher mainly because the seminar gave them practice with the final assessment’s format.●
YOU GOT IT WRONG.BUT DO YOU KNOW WHY?
THE MISTAKE IS A CLUE
AThe seminar’s benefits were concentrated among students who had scored highly in the introductory course.×
UNSUPPORTED INFERENCE
The passage does not state that the seminar’s benefits were concentrated among high scorers.
Supported by the passage
CThe better results of regular attenders may partly reflect the stronger preparation of those who chose to attend.✓

BIRBAL FEEDBACK

WHY IT FAILED

The passage does not state that the seminar’s benefits were concentrated among high scorers; it only notes that more high scorers attended.

THE TRAP

It references the high-scoring students and the seminar’s benefits, which are both mentioned in the passage.

Trap: Unsupported Inference.

WHAT TO DO NEXT TIME

Keep the conclusion within what the passage supports; plausibility alone is not evidence.
